GRAMMAR WORKSHOP

I am / My name is

Notice the pattern → practise → personalise.

1 · NOTICE

A: Hello! I’m ____. B: Hello! My name is ____. A: Nice to meet you. B: Nice to meet you, too.

Underline the part that changes. Say the examples aloud.

2 · PRACTICE
1. Complete: I ___ a student. 2. Complete: My name ___ Lina. 3. Make a question: You are new. → ___ you new? 4. Make it negative: I am late. → I ___ late.
⚙️ GRAMMAR RULE
Positive I am / You are / He is / She is
Negative I’m not / You aren’t / He isn’t / She isn’t
Question Am I? / Are you? / Is he/she?
Short answer Yes, I am. / No, I’m not.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKES
✗ I am student. → ✓ I am a student.
✗ I have 16 years. → ✓ I am 16.
✗ What your name? → ✓ What is your name?

A Simple Classroom

Hello! My name is Lina. I am a student. This is my book. This is my teacher. I say hello when I arrive and goodbye when I leave. In class, I listen, repeat and open my book. I can say my name and use a few English classroom words. The teacher smiles and checks that everyone has a book and a pen. Lina repeats the classroom words with the group. She learns that simple English can help her communicate from the first lesson. Before the class ends, Lina writes three new words in her notebook: “listen,” “repeat,” and “open.” She practises saying them with Adam. When she forgets the last word, she asks Adam to repeat it slowly. She then points to the door and uses “open” and “close” correctly. By the end of the lesson, Lina feels more confident because she can greet someone, give basic information and understand several simple instructions.

A Learner Form

My name is Samir. I am sixteen. My first name is Samir and my surname is Benali. I live in Oran. My phone number has ten digits. I can spell my name slowly. On a learner form, I write my name, age, city and country. Samir checks the spelling of his surname and the final digit of his phone number before handing in the form. He writes his phone number in groups of two digits and asks how to spell the city name. His partner reads the numbers aloud and Samir checks them. They discover that saying each number slowly makes the information easier to understand. The teacher collects the forms and asks the class to check one final detail: the difference between a number and a letter. Samir answers correctly and checks the final digit once more. Samir practises both with his partner. He realises that careful repetition is useful, especially when he gives personal information on the phone or fills in a form. The form is now complete, and Samir feels ready to use the same questions in another conversation. He thanks his partner and puts his notebook away.
